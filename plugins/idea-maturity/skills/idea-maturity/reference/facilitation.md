# Running the maturity check with a group

## Contents
- Session sequence
- Who needs to be in the room
- Comparing several ideas
- Common failure modes in the room

Read this when the request is to facilitate a session or review a backlog, rather than assess a single idea.

## Session sequence

1. **Go bottom-up.** Start at stage 1, not at the solution. Groups want to start at the solution because that is the part they are proud of.
2. **Stop at the first stage** that cannot be honestly affirmed. That is the status. Do not negotiate it.
3. **Do not skip.** Work on stage 4 while stage 2 is open does not count toward the status, and saying so early prevents an hour of defense.
4. **Record what is missing** for the next stage, how it will be obtained, and who obtains it. A gap without a name attached is not closed.

The point of the sequence is that the discussion stops being "good idea or bad idea" and becomes "what is still missing". That is what removes the politics.

## Who needs to be in the room

The status is only determined honestly if the room is not just the idea's authors:

- someone with **data access** — can check baselines and reach on the spot
- someone in **direct user contact** — support, research, sales
- someone from **delivery** — can judge stage 4 realistically
- someone who **did not co-develop the idea** — otherwise there is no real counter-question

If the fourth is missing, the session produces a status that everyone already agreed on before it started.

## Comparing several ideas

Two prioritizations run in parallel, and mixing them is what stalls backlogs.

**What to build.** Only among ideas that have cleared stage 5. They compete on impact against effort. Anything below stage 5 is not a candidate yet — it is a question, not an option.

**What to mature next.** This is the one teams forget, and the one that actually binds: discovery capacity is as scarce as build capacity. Sort the candidates by two values:

- **status** — which stage it has fully reached
- **estimated impact** — from stage 2, roughly reach × severity

Then spend discovery where the gap to the next stage is cheapest and the sized problem is largest. Several ideas blocked at stage 2 by the same missing baseline need one measurement, not three debates — that is usually the highest-yield move available in a review.

Ideas at the same stage compete. Ideas at different stages do not: the lower one needs evidence, not a decision. Groups reliably get this wrong — they compare a stage-4 idea against a stage-1 idea and conclude the stage-1 idea is weak, when it is simply unassessed.

**The funnel narrows on purpose.** Not every idea should reach stage 5. Killing one at stage 2 costs a measurement; killing it after launch costs a quarter. A review that ends with nothing eliminated has usually avoided the decision rather than made it.

## Common failure modes in the room

- **The status gets negotiated upward** because a deadline already exists. Note it as *"Stufe [n], Umsetzung läuft trotzdem"* — that is a legitimate business decision, but it must be recorded as one, not disguised as maturity.
- **The loudest evidence wins.** A vivid interview quote outweighs an ambiguous number. Ratings exist to prevent this: one interview is *reported*, not *validated*.
- **The causal chain is skipped** because it feels obvious. It is the chain, not the endpoints, where the guessing hides.
- **Everything is declared blocking.** Exactly one blocking stage; the rest is downstream.
