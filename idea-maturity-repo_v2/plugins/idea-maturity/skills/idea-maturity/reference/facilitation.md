# Running it with a group

## Contents
- Session sequence
- Who needs to be in the room
- Comparing several initiatives
- Common failure modes

Read this when the request is to facilitate a session or review a backlog, rather than assess a single initiative.

## Session sequence

1. **Go bottom-up.** Start at stage 0, not at the solution. Groups want to start at the solution because that is the part they are proud of.
2. **Stop at the first stage** that cannot be honestly affirmed. That is the status. Do not negotiate it.
3. **Do not skip.** Work on stage 4 while stage 2 is open does not count toward the status, and saying so early prevents an hour of defence.
4. **Record what is missing** for the next stage, how it will be obtained, and who obtains it. A gap without a name attached is not closed.

The point is that the discussion stops being "good idea or bad idea" and becomes "what is still missing". That is what removes the politics.

## Who needs to be in the room

The status is only determined honestly if the room is not just the initiative's authors:

- someone with **data access** — can check baselines and reach on the spot
- someone in **direct user contact** — support, research, sales
- someone from **delivery** — can size stage 5 realistically
- someone who **did not co-develop it** — otherwise there is no real counter-question

If the fourth is missing, the session produces a status everyone agreed on before it started.

## Comparing several initiatives

Two prioritizations run in parallel, and mixing them is what stalls backlogs.

**What to build.** Only among initiatives with a `build` judgement at stage 5. They compete on value against effort. Anything below is not a candidate yet — it is a question, not an option.

**What to mature next.** The one teams forget, and the one that actually binds: discovery capacity is as scarce as build capacity. Sort by `stage` and `impact_amount`, then spend discovery where the gap to the next stage is cheapest and the sized problem largest. Several initiatives blocked at stage 2 by the same missing baseline need one measurement, not three debates.

Initiatives at the same stage compete. At different stages they do not: the lower one needs evidence, not a decision. Groups reliably get this wrong — they compare a stage-4 initiative against a stage-1 initiative and conclude the latter is weak, when it is simply unassessed.

**Obligatory demand is deducted first,** as a capacity block with a deadline. Only the remainder is prioritized. Otherwise every deadline beats every idea and the ranking becomes a formality.

**The funnel narrows on purpose.** A review that ends with nothing eliminated has usually avoided the decision rather than made it.

## Common failure modes

- **The status gets negotiated upward** because a deadline already exists. Record it as *"stage [n], proceeding regardless"* — a legitimate business decision, but it must be recorded as one, not disguised as maturity.
- **The loudest evidence wins.** A vivid interview quote outweighs an ambiguous number. The ratings exist to prevent this: one interview is `reported`, not `validated`.
- **The causal chain is skipped** because it feels obvious. It is the chain, not the endpoints, where the guessing hides.
- **A solution enters the briefing.** Someone names an artefact in stage 1, 2 or 3. Move it to stage 4 and continue; the intent must survive it being discarded.
- **Everything is declared blocking.** Exactly one blocking stage; the rest is downstream.
